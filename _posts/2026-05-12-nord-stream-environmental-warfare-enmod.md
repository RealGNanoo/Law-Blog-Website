**STOCKHOLM** — The sabotage of underwater gas pipelines and subsea fiber-optic telecommunications links in international waters has brought maritime critical infrastructure protection to the forefront of international humanitarian law.

Beyond the immediate geopolitical impact, these events expose regulatory and evidentiary thresholds in treaties regulating environmental harm and the targeting of dual-use infrastructure during armed conflict.

---

## 1. Additional Protocol I: The Dual-Use Standard

Under Article 52(2) of Additional Protocol I (API) to the 1949 Geneva Conventions, civilian objects become legitimate military objectives only if:

1. By their nature, location, purpose, or use, they make an effective contribution to military action; and
2. Their total or partial destruction, capture, or neutralization offers a definite military advantage in the circumstances ruling at the time.

Underwater pipelines and commercial communication cables are dual-use infrastructure. While commercial gas powers civilian industries and heating grids, it may also supply state defense facilities. Under the customary principle of proportionality (API Article 51(5)(b)), an attack is unlawful if it is expected to cause incidental loss of civilian life, injury to civilians, or damage to civilian objects that would be excessive in relation to the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated.

---

## 2. The Environmental Threshold: ENMOD vs. Protocol I

When subsea energy infrastructure ruptures and releases large quantities of methane into marine ecosystems, two specialized treaty frameworks apply:

| Statutory Regime | Environmental Threshold | Scope of Prohibition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Additional Protocol I (Arts. 35(3) & 55)** | Cumulative: "Widespread, long-term, and severe damage" | High threshold; damage historically understood to endure across decades. |
| **ENMOD Convention (1976)** | Disjunctive: "Widespread, long-lasting, or severe effects" | Prohibits deliberate manipulation of natural processes as a method of warfare. |

Because the threshold under API Article 35(3) is cumulative and demanding, establishing that instantaneous methane discharges or localized seabed disturbances meet the statutory definition of environmental warfare remains an acute doctrinal obstacle.
