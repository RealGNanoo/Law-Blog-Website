**GENEVA** — The paralysis of the World Trade Organization’s Appellate Body, triggered in December 2019 when blocking of member appointments left the tribunal without a quorum, has surpassed five years of institutional deadlock. With the formal two-tier dispute settlement mechanism under Article 17 of the Dispute Settlement Understanding (DSU) inoperative, losing respondents in panel proceedings have frequently chosen to "appeal into the void"—filing appeals that cannot be heard, thereby preventing panel reports from attaining legal finality.

In response, a coalition of WTO members led by the European Union, China, Brazil, Canada, and others operationalized the Multi-Party Interim Appeal Arbitration Arrangement (MPIA) under Article 25 of the DSU. As the arrangement handles an increasing share of disputes, its practical operation reveals both procedural adaptations and systemic jurisdictional limits within multilateral economic law.

---

## 1. Statutory Architecture Under DSU Article 25

Article 25 of the DSU provides that "expeditious arbitration within the WTO as an alternative means of dispute settlement can facilitate the solution of certain disputes which concern issues that are typically well defined by both parties." 

While historically utilized only once prior to 2020 (*US – Section 110(5) Copyright Act*), the MPIA repurposed this provision into an interim surrogate for appellate review. Participating members enter into bilateral agreed procedures at the panel stage, binding themselves to refer panel findings to an MPIA arbitral pool rather than letting an appeal languish on an empty Appellate Body docket.

Key structural adaptations under the MPIA include:

* **Voluntary Plurilateral Reach:** Because the arrangement is rooted in consensual arbitration, it binds only signatories. Non-participating members—most notably the United States—remain outside the framework, maintaining the risk of unappealable deadlocks in disputes involving non-signatories.
* **Streamlined Timelines:** Addressing long-standing critiques regarding the Appellate Body exceeding the 90-day review limit under DSU Article 17.5, the MPIA empowers arbitrators to implement page limits, restrict hearings, and exclude issues deemed unnecessary to resolve the dispute.
* **Panel Fact-Finding Deference:** Arbitrators have largely adhered to a strict standard of review under DSU Article 11, limiting interventions to clear errors of legal interpretation rather than re-evaluating factual determinations made by first-instance panels.

---

## 2. Institutional Divergence and Systemic Jurisprudence

The operational coexistence of the MPIA alongside the suspended official Appellate Body has prompted debate over the unity of WTO case law:

| Dispute Settlement Dimension | Statutory DSU Article 17 (Appellate Body) | MPIA Article 25 Framework |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Basis** | Standing multilateral organ established by the Dispute Settlement Body. | Plurilateral arbitration based on mutual consent of participating parties. |
| **Enforceability** | Reports adopted automatically by reverse consensus under Article 17.14. | Arbitral awards notified to the DSB and enforceable under DSU Articles 21 and 22. |
| **Doctrinal Precedent** | Expectations of "cogent reasons" before departing from prior Appellate Body jurisprudence. | Non-binding on non-signatories; awards inform future panels only as persuasive authority. |

Arbitrators serving on MPIA disputes (*such as Colombia – Frozen Fries* and subsequent reviews) have generally respected prior Appellate Body acquis, avoiding radical normative departures. This institutional conservatism has prevented a direct doctrinal rupture. 

However, because arbitral awards do not technically carry the institutional endorsement of the entire WTO membership through the formal DSB adoption process, third parties continue to contest whether MPIA reasoning can constitute subsequent practice establishing the agreement of the parties under Article 31(3)(b) of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (VCLT).

---

## 3. Structural Limits and Prospects for Reform

The MPIA was conceived as a temporary safeguard pending the full restoration of a binding, two-tier system. Its endurance has instead highlighted structural realities of contemporary international trade relations:

1. **Bifurcation of the Trading System:** Global trade disputes are increasingly partitioned into two distinct procedural tracks: disputes among MPIA signatories that reach binding resolution, and disputes involving non-participants that end in unresolved panel appeals.
2. **Substantive Scope Constraints:** Consensual arbitration functions most reliably in conventional tariff, anti-dumping, and subsidy disputes. Where cases touch on broad national security exceptions under GATT Article XXI or unilateral industrial policy subsidies, sovereign states remain hesitant to delegate final review to expedited arbitral panels.

While the MPIA has preserved the rule of law among its participants, it remains an imperfect procedural patch. Full resolution of the dispute settlement crisis continues to hinge on multilateral negotiations over the scope of judicial review, standards of deference, and the formal authority of the WTO adjudicative mechanism.
