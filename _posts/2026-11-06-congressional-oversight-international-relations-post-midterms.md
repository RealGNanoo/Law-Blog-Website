**WASHINGTON** — The conclusion of the 2026 midterm elections and the resulting realignment in Congress introduces immediate institutional friction into United States foreign policy. While the executive branch retains primary initiative under Article II of the US Constitution in conducting foreign affairs, Congress holds the decisive purse strings, statutory oversight mechanisms, and constitutional trade authorities that determine the endurance of international commitments.

With legislative power recalibrated, the intersection of domestic statutory constraints and international legal obligations faces scrutiny across three primary theaters: non-adjudicated economic statecraft, War Powers limits in the Middle East, and ongoing security commitments in Eastern Europe.

---

## 1. Trade Policy and Section 301 Delegations

Over recent legislative cycles, the executive branch has relied heavily on broad delegations of authority under the Trade Act of 1974 (specifically Section 301) and the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) to impose unilateral tariffs and outbound investment restrictions. 

These measures have operated largely outside the dispute settlement system of the World Trade Organization, where the Appellate Body remains inquorate. However, a shifted congressional balance alters the domestic viability of unilateral trade policy:

* **Statutory Sunset and Reauthorization:** Discretionary trade tools and bilateral preferential arrangements require ongoing legislative funding or tacit committee approval. Enhanced oversight in the House Ways and Means and Senate Finance Committees will likely challenge the expansive interpretation of "national security" rationales used to justify tariff walls.
* **Supply Chain and Environmental Measures:** Alignment with trading partners—specifically concerning carbon border adjustments and critical mineral agreements—demands statutory permanence. Without affirmative legislation, executive agreements with the European Union and Asian partners remain precarious administrative compacts, vulnerable to immediate domestic litigation under the Administrative Procedure Act.

## 2. The Iran File and War Powers Resolution Constraints

In the Persian Gulf, escalating maritime interdictions and non-state proxy engagements have heightened the risk of kinetic confrontation. Under international law, unilateral military action outside the United Nations Security Council framework must strictly satisfy the criteria of self-defense under Article 51 of the UN Charter—necessity, proportionality, and an imminent armed attack.

Domestically, the legislative branch retains the constitutional prerogative to declare war:

* **Reasserting the 1973 War Powers Resolution:** Incoming congressional leadership is positioned to demand strict compliance with statutory reporting mandates under Section 4(a)(1) whenever US armed forces are introduced into hostilities. 
* **Scrutiny of Statutory Authorizations:** Decades-old Authorizations for Use of Military Force (AUMFs) have historically been stretched to cover modern asymmetric confrontations. Renewed legislative pressure to repeal or narrow these authorizations constrains the executive's capacity to launch retaliatory strikes without prior congressional debate, directly impacting deterrence calculations in the Strait of Hormuz.

---

## 3. Ukraine, Security Assistance, and Frozen Sovereign Asset Mechanisms

Support for Ukraine and the enforcement of countermeasures against the Russian Federation remain anchored in multilateral consensus, yet the mechanics of that support depend entirely on congressional appropriations.

Beyond direct military aid packages, the central international legal controversy concerns the repurposing of immobilized Russian sovereign assets:

* **The Scope of Countermeasures:** Under the International Law Commission's Articles on State Responsibility (ARSIWA), countermeasures taken by non-injured third parties to enforce obligations *erga omnes* must remain temporary and reversible. Outright confiscation of foreign central bank reserves pushes customary international law beyond accepted doctrine.
* **The REPO Act in Practice:** While the Rebuilding Economic Prosperity and Opportunity for Ukrainians Act provided domestic statutory authority to seize designated sovereign assets, the operational rollout has stalled due to sovereign immunity protections recognized across European jurisdictions. A newly configured Congress will dictate whether Washington leans into bilateral confiscation mechanisms or maintains parity with G7 consensus to prevent asymmetric retaliatory risks against dollar-denominated reserves globally.

---

## The Broader Doctrinal Trajectory

The post-midterm legislative configuration underscores a structural reality of modern international relations: foreign policy doctrine cannot be analyzed in a domestic vacuum. When legislative assemblies reassert oversight, the executive's ability to navigate customary international law, deploy economic sanctions, and sustain alliance commitments faces continuous domestic statutory checks.
